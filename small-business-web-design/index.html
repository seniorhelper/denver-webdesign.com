The thing you keep running into

This isn't a criticism of them. A twenty-person agency has an office, account managers, project managers and a sales team. Why this matters more than the price

Small business owners tend to evaluate a website as a cost. The better question is how many customers it needs to bring in before it pays for itself, and for most trades that number is startlingly low.

A remodeler with a $6,000 average job needs one extra customer roughly every five years to cover $99 a month. A restaurant with a $40 ticket needs about thirty extra covers a month. Those are very different propositions, and it's worth knowing which one you are before you spend anything.

It also works in reverse. If you run the numbers and a website couldn't realistically move them — because your work comes entirely from referral, or nobody searches for what you do — that's genuinely useful to know. We'd rather you spent the money somewhere it works.

The five that actually cost you money

Mistakes we see constantly

Every service crammed onto one page

The most common and most expensive mistake. If you do bathrooms, kitchens and basements, that's three searches, three sets of competitors and three different customers. One page listing all three competes properly for none of them. Splitting them is usually the single highest-return change available to a small business site.

The phone number as an image

Sometimes done to avoid scrapers. The effect is that nobody can tap it, and search engines and AI assistants can't read it at all. If your number isn't machine-readable text, you're invisible in exactly the situations where somebody is ready to call.

Copy written to sound like a bigger company

"Delivering integrated solutions with a commitment to excellence." Nobody searched for that. Being small is often the reason people choose you — they get the owner rather than a call centre. Writing like a corporation throws away your actual advantage.

No prices anywhere, not even a range

The most common reason someone leaves a small business site is not knowing whether they can afford you. You don't need a full price list. A starting figure, a typical range, or a worked example removes the biggest source of hesitation on the page.

Built once and never touched again

Hours change, services change, the phone number changes. A site that contradicts reality costs you trust the moment somebody notices. This is why we include updates rather than billing hourly for them — the alternative is you not bothering, which helps nobody.

Small business web design questions

How much should a small business website cost?

Independent 2026 research puts most professional small business builds between $3,000 and $15,000, with freelancers typically $3,000 to $8,000 and agencies $8,000 to $15,000. Our custom-coded sites start at $99 a month with no setup fee, and builder plans start at $5.99 a month. Rates rise with size and complexity.

Is my business too small to need a website?

Depends entirely on whether people search for what you sell. A remodeler, a dentist or a plumber has real search demand. A business selling to twelve companies in the state does not, and would be better served by a credible one-page site than by chasing rankings. Work out what one extra customer is worth to you first.

Do you have a minimum project size?

No. A three-page site for a sole trader is normal work here. That's the main structural difference between us and an agency — low overhead means small projects still make sense.

How many pages does a small business site need?

Usually one per service you sell, plus a home page, an about page and a contact page. So a three-service business is looking at around six pages. More than that is often padding; fewer usually means services are sharing a page and competing with each other.

Can I update it myself?

On our monthly plans updates are included — send us the change and it usually happens the same day, which is faster than most people manage themselves. If you'd rather have the keys, say so at the start and we'll build on something that supports it.

What if I don't have photos or written content?

Most small business owners don't, and waiting for it is what stalls website projects. We write the content from a fifteen minute phone call and you correct it. For photos, your phone is genuinely fine — real pictures of real work beat stock every time.

Will it work on phones?

Every site is designed at phone width first, because that's where nearly all local searches happen. You can check any website yourself with Google's free PageSpeed Insights tool, including whoever else you're considering hiring.

How fast can it be live?

Some single-page sites go live within 24 hours. A typical small business site with a handful of service pages takes about a week. The thing that moves the timeline is how quickly you can answer questions, not how fast the code gets written.

What happens after it launches?

Hosting, updates, security and edits are included on the monthly plans, and the site gets submitted to Google Search Console so it starts being crawled. New service, new number, new photos — send it over. One number to call.
