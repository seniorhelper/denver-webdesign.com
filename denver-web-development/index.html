The things people actually hire a developer for

Nobody wakes up wanting custom development. They want a specific problem to stop costing them time. These are the ones that come up most.

Booking and scheduling that reflects reality

Off-the-shelf booking widgets take appointments your calendar can't actually honour. Custom scheduling checks real availability, blocks travel time between jobs, handles your service areas differently, and stops double-booking before it happens.

Who needs it: contractors, clinics, salons, inspectors, anyone whose day is a route rather than a list.

Instant quotes and price calculators

Square footage, room count, material choice, distance — whatever drives your pricing, put it on the page and let the customer get a number in ten seconds. This is one of the highest-converting things a service business can add, because "call for a quote" loses the people who won't call.

Who needs it: remodelers, movers, landscapers, roofers, flooring, anyone competing on transparency.

Ecommerce website development

Selling online means inventory, shipping rules, tax, payment security and abandoned carts. We build on Shopify when the store is standard and custom when it isn't — unusual product configurations, wholesale pricing tiers, or bundles no platform handles cleanly.

Denver web development questions

What does a web developer do?

A web developer writes the code that makes a website work — the forms, the calculations, the connections to other software, the parts that respond when somebody clicks. A web designer decides how it looks. On small business projects the same person often does both, which avoids a handoff where things get lost.

How much does custom web development cost in Denver?

It depends almost entirely on what the site has to do. A brochure site with a contact form sits at the bottom of the range — ours start at $99 per month. Booking systems, calculators and portals are quoted per project because the scope genuinely varies. Denver agencies commonly quote $9,000 to $22,000 for a professional five-page site before any custom functionality is added.

How long does web development take?

Some single-page sites go live within 24 hours. A multi-page business site takes seven days or more. Ecommerce stores usually run two to four weeks. Custom applications and mobile apps take 30 to 60 days and often longer. The variable that moves the timeline most is how quickly decisions get made, not how fast the code gets written.

Do I need a custom web application or just a website?

Ask what happens if you don't build it. If the answer is "someone on my team keeps doing that by hand for six hours a week," an application probably pays for itself. If the answer is "nothing changes," it's a want rather than a need. Most small businesses are better served by a fast, findable website than by software.

Can you work with my existing site instead of rebuilding it?

Often yes. If the site already ranks and converts reasonably, adding functionality to it beats starting over — you keep the search history you've built. If it's on something unmaintainable or fundamentally slow, a rebuild usually costs less over two years than continuous patching.

What technology do you build with?

Static HTML, CSS and JavaScript by default, because it's the fastest and most secure option for most business sites. WordPress when you need to publish content yourself. Shopify for standard ecommerce. Custom back-end work when the requirement genuinely calls for it. We pick based on what the project needs, not on what we feel like using.

Will I be able to update the site myself?

Depends what you want to change and which platform fits. On our monthly plans, updates are included — you send us the change and we make it, usually the same day. If you'd rather have the keys and do it yourself, say so up front and we'll build on something that supports that.

Do you work with freelance and contract clients?

Yes. Plenty of our work is a single defined project rather than an ongoing relationship — adding a feature, fixing something broken, building one tool. You don't have to sign up for anything monthly to get a specific problem solved.
