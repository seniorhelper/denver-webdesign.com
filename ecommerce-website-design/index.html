Four routes

Which platform your store should live on

We build on all of these and we'll tell you which fits. Anyone who recommends the same platform to every client is telling you about themselves, not about your business.

Most stores

Shopify

Purpose-built for selling. Payments, inventory, tax, shipping and abandoned carts all handled by people whose entire job is handling them. You pay a monthly fee and you don't think about card security again.

  • Fastest to launch, usually 2–4 weeks
  • Payment security is their problem, not yours
  • Huge app ecosystem when you need something extra
  • Monthly fee forever, plus app subscriptions
  • Less control over the underlying code

More on Shopify design →

Content-heavy stores

WooCommerce on WordPress

Sensible when the store sits inside a content site you're already publishing to. The core plugin is free, but the extensions add up — shipping and inventory add-ons typically run $100 to $300 a year.

  • You own everything and can move it
  • Blends with an existing WordPress site
  • Security and updates are now your responsibility
  • Plugin conflicts are a real ongoing cost

Unusual requirements

Custom build

For product configurations, wholesale pricing tiers or bundles that no platform handles cleanly. Published research puts full custom ecommerce builds at $45,000 to $250,000 depending on catalogue complexity and integrations.

  • Does exactly what your business needs
  • No platform fees taking a cut forever
  • Far higher upfront cost
  • You're responsible for everything, including payment security
Worth considering first

Marketplaces and payment links

Etsy, eBay, Amazon, or just a payment link on a normal website. Not glamorous, and for a business testing whether anyone wants the product it's often the smartest first move.

  • Almost no upfront cost
  • Built-in audience already searching
  • They own the customer relationship, not you
  • Commission is usually higher than card processing

Biggest leak

Shipping cost revealed at the end

The single most reliable way to lose a cart. Say what shipping costs before checkout, or set a free-shipping threshold and put it at the top of every page.

Biggest leak

Forcing account creation

Guest checkout exists for a reason. Asking someone to invent a password to buy a $30 item loses a meaningful share of them for nothing.

Ecommerce website questions

How much does an ecommerce website cost?

A standard Shopify build sits in normal website territory and we quote it per project. Full custom ecommerce at scale runs $45,000 to $250,000 depending on catalogue complexity and integrations, according to published 2026 research. Most stores don't need the second thing, and it's worth asking hard before anyone quotes you it.

Shopify or WooCommerce?

Shopify if selling is the main event and you want payment security handled for you. WooCommerce if the store sits inside a WordPress content site you already publish to and you want to own everything. The trade is convenience versus control, and the ongoing maintenance burden is genuinely different between them.

How long does it take to build an online store?

Usually two to four weeks. The variable is almost never the build — it's product data. Photos, descriptions, weights, variants and prices for every item you sell. Stores with fifty products and no photos take much longer than the code suggests.

What are the ongoing costs of an online store?

Platform subscription, card processing on every order at typically 2.9% plus 30 cents, and app or plugin subscriptions. Ecommerce extensions on WooCommerce commonly run $100 to $300 a year on top. Run your own numbers through the calculator above — the per-transaction fee surprises people with low order values.

Can you migrate my existing store?

Usually. The important part is protecting product URLs that already rank, because losing those loses the traffic that was already buying. Same principle as any website redesign — inventory first, map every address, redirect at launch.

Do I need to worry about sales tax?

Yes, and it's genuinely complicated once you sell across state lines. Platforms handle the calculation, but the filing and the thresholds are your responsibility. This is a question for an accountant rather than a web designer, and anyone who waves it away is doing you a disservice.

How do I get my products found in search?

Product schema so price and availability show in results, unique descriptions rather than the manufacturer's copy, fast product pages, and category pages that answer how people actually search. Duplicate supplier descriptions across a thousand stores is the most common reason product pages don't rank.

Should I sell on my own site or a marketplace?

Both, usually. Marketplaces bring an audience already searching, which is worth a lot early. Your own store keeps the customer relationship and the margin. If you're testing whether anyone wants the product at all, starting on a marketplace is often smarter than building anything.

Do you work with stores outside Denver?

Yes. Ecommerce has no geography — your customers are wherever they are. Most of our clients are in the Denver metro, and we also work along the Front Range including Monument and Colorado Springs, plus out of state.

What if I only sell a few items?

Then a full store is probably the wrong purchase. A normal website with payment links or an invoice covers a handful of orders a month without a monthly platform fee. We'd rather set that up and revisit when volume justifies the upgrade.
