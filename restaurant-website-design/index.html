What a PDF menu costs you

It opens in a separate viewer. It loads at document speed rather than page speed. It's sized for paper, so every dish needs pinching and dragging to read.

  • Highest bounce rate of any single element on a restaurant site
  • Search engines can't read the dish names, so you don't rank for them
  • AI assistants can't quote your menu when somebody asks what's good nearby
  • Updating a price means re-exporting and re-uploading a whole file
  • No way to link straight to "Mains" from a Google result

What a live menu does instead

Real text on a real page. It appears instantly, scrolls with a thumb, and every dish name is something Google and ChatGPT can actually read.

  • Loads with the page — no download, no viewer, no wait
  • Dish names become searchable, so "cacio e pepe Denver" can find you
  • Today's hours and your rating sit above the menu where they're wanted
  • Order and call buttons stay visible while people scroll
  • Change a price in ten seconds without touching a design file

Not ready to rebuild the whole site yet? A live digital menu customers can tap open solves most of this on its own, and it works alongside whatever site you already have.

The order it happens in

What a hungry person does in ninety seconds

Thumb order

They're deciding between you and two other places. Everything below has to be answered before they lose patience, and in roughly this sequence.

1

Are you open?

Not a seven-day table — today. "Open until 10pm" answers the question instantly; a grid makes them work out what day it is and find the right row. Holiday exceptions matter more than anything else on the site during a holiday.

2

What's the food, and what does it cost?

The menu, as text, immediately. Prices included. A menu without prices creates the exact hesitation you're trying to remove, and people assume expensive when you hide it.

3

Is it any good?

Your rating and review count, visible early. Real photos of your actual food, not stock. Ten honest photos of real dishes beat three beautiful fake ones, and people can tell the difference immediately.

4

How do I get it?

Order, book, call or directions — whichever applies to you, visible without scrolling on a phone. A phone number that isn't a tappable link is a phone number nobody calls.

Restaurant website questions

FAQ
Why shouldn't my menu be a PDF?

Three reasons. It opens in a separate viewer and loads slowly, which is why PDF menus correlate with the highest bounce rate of any single element on a restaurant site. Search engines can't read the dish names properly, so you don't rank for them. And AI assistants can't quote your menu when somebody asks what's good nearby. A live HTML menu fixes all three.

How much does a restaurant website cost?

Our custom-coded sites start at $99 a month with no setup fee, including hosting, updates and search structure. Full agency builds in Denver commonly run $9,000 to $22,000. A restaurant site doesn't need to be complicated — it needs to be fast, mobile-first and honest about hours and prices.

Should I put prices on the menu?

Yes. Hiding prices creates exactly the hesitation you're trying to remove, and people assume expensive when they can't see. It also gives search engines and AI assistants something concrete to work with when someone searches for a price range in your area.

Do I need online ordering on my own site?

It's worth it once you have enough direct regulars that third-party commission is a real cost. Early on, a clear phone number and a link to whatever platform you already use is fine. Don't build ordering before you have people who'd use it.

How important is mobile?

It's the whole thing. Roughly three quarters of restaurant traffic is a phone, frequently outdoors on patchy signal. Designing for desktop first is designing for the wrong audience. Sub-two-second loading on mobile is the baseline, not the goal.

Can I update the menu myself?

On our monthly plans you send us the change and it's usually live the same day, which is faster than most owners manage themselves between shifts. If you change specials daily and want direct control, say so and we'll build it so you can.

Do I need professional food photography?

It helps a lot and it's the highest-return visual investment a restaurant can make. But real photos taken on a decent phone beat stock every time. Ten honest shots of your actual dishes outperform three beautiful generic ones, because people recognise stock instantly.

How long does a restaurant website take to build?

About a week for most, sometimes faster. The variable is almost always the menu — getting every dish, description and price in a usable format. If you have that ready, the build moves quickly.
