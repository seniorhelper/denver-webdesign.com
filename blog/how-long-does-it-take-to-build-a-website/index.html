How long does it take to build a website? An honest timeline

The coding is rarely what takes the time. Decisions, content and feedback rounds are. Here's how long each phase usually runs, what stretches it, and how to get to launch sooner.

8 min read · By Zach Wennstedt · Updated October 9, 2026

Short answer: A simple small business website usually takes two to six weeks from kickoff to launch. A larger custom site with many pages or features takes two to four months, and an online store or custom web app can take longer. Content and decisions, not coding, cause most delays.

"How long will it take?" is the second question everyone asks, right after price. The honest answer is that it depends far more on you than on the builder. That's not a dodge. It's the most useful thing to know before you start, because it means you control most of the timeline.

The phases, and how long each one takes

1. Discovery (a few days to a week). What the site needs to do, who it's for, which services get their own pages, and what success looks like. Skipping this step is the most expensive way to save a week.

2. Content (one to four weeks). Words and photos. This is the phase that most often stalls, because it lands on the business owner's desk next to everything else. If you can only speed up one thing, speed up this.

3. Design (one to three weeks). Layout and look, usually starting with the homepage and one inner page. Each round of feedback adds a few days; fewer, clearer rounds move faster than many small ones.

4. Build (one to four weeks). The actual coding. For a small site built by an experienced team, this is often the fastest phase. Stores, booking systems and integrations take longer.

5. Testing (a few days). Phones, browsers, forms, speed, and a crawl to catch broken links. Rushing this is how sites launch with forms that go nowhere.

6. Launch and settle (a week or two). Redirects from the old site, the sitemap submitted to Google, and a watch on Search Console. If you're replacing an existing site, read our redesign checklist first.

The real bottleneck

On most small business projects, the build waits on content far longer than content waits on the build. Have your service descriptions, photos and a list of common customer questions ready before kickoff and you can take weeks off the timeline.

What makes a website take longer

  • Content written during the build. Writing while designing means designing twice.
  • Too many decision makers. Every extra approver adds a feedback round.
  • Features decided late. Adding booking or a store halfway through changes the structure.
  • Waiting on access. Domain logins, hosting and old analytics that nobody can find.
  • Migrating a big old site. Mapping hundreds of old URLs takes care, and it's worth it.

How to launch sooner

  • Gather logins for your domain, hosting and Google accounts before the first meeting.
  • Pick one person to give feedback and make final calls.
  • Write a rough version of each service page yourself. A builder can polish rough text much faster than they can invent it.
  • Launch the core pages first, then add the rest. A good site that's live beats a perfect site that isn't.

Some simple sites can go live within days when the content is ready and the scope is small. Larger builds are better planned in phases. If you're comparing quotes, ask each builder for a phase-by-phase timeline and what they need from you at each step. The answer tells you a lot about how the project will run.

Common questions

How long does it take to build a small business website?

Usually two to six weeks from kickoff to launch for a simple site, provided content and feedback arrive on time. Larger custom sites take two to four months.

What slows down a website project the most?

Content. Text and photos written during the build, plus long feedback rounds with several decision makers, cause most delays. The coding itself is rarely the bottleneck.

Can a website be built in a week?

A small, simple site can be live within days when the content is ready and the scope is tight. Stores, booking systems and larger sites take longer.

How long does an ecommerce website take?

Typically longer than a brochure site, often one to three months, depending on the number of products, payment and shipping setup, and integrations.
