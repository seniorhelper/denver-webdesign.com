Local search

For a local business, your Google profile may matter more than your website

If your customers are local and searching on a phone, there's a good chance they never scroll far enough to see a website at all. The map pack is right at the top, and it's fed by something free that most businesses fill in once and forget.

6 min read · Denver Web Design

Search a service and a town on your phone. Look at what's above the ordinary blue links. On most local searches you'll find a map with three businesses, phone buttons, ratings and directions.

That block is the first thing a customer sees, and often the only thing they interact with. Getting into it is a different job from ranking a website, and for a lot of trades it's the more valuable one.

Why the profile can outperform the site

A profile answers the questions a ready-to-buy customer has without them visiting anything. Are you open now. How far away are you. What do other people say. What's the number.

Somebody standing in a flooded kitchen isn't reading your about page. They're tapping the call button on whichever plumber looks closest and least alarming. Your website plays no part in that decision.

What actually moves it

Google weighs three broad things for local results: how close you are to the searcher, how well you match what they asked for, and how prominent you appear. You can't change the first one. The other two are largely within your control.

  1. Complete every fieldNot most of them. Hours, categories, services, service areas, description, attributes. Incomplete profiles underperform complete ones consistently, and filling it in costs nothing but an afternoon.
  2. Get the primary category rightThis is one of the strongest signals and people pick it carelessly. It should describe what you primarily are, not everything you can do. Secondary categories cover the rest.
  3. Photos, regularlyReal photos of real work, added over time rather than twenty on day one. A profile that shows recent activity looks like a business that still exists.
  4. Reviews, steadilyVolume and recency both matter, and a steady trickle beats a burst. Ask every satisfied customer, and reply to every review including the unhappy ones — that reply is read by future customers more than the review itself.
  5. Name, address and phone identical everywhereExactly the same on your site, your profile and every directory. "Suite 4" in one place and "Ste. 4" in another is the kind of inconsistency that quietly costs you.

Where the website still earns its place

None of this makes a site optional. The profile gets you seen; the site is what people check before they trust you with money. For anything above a certain price, they will look.

The two also feed each other. Your site's content and structure help Google understand what your business actually does, which affects how it matches you to searches. A profile pointing at a slow, thin website is working against itself.

The order that makes sense

If you have neither, do the profile first. It's free, it's faster, and for most local trades it produces enquiries sooner. Then build the site that turns those enquiries into customers. Doing it the other way round is common and slower.

The mistakes that cost the most

Unclaimed profiles. Plenty of businesses have a listing generated automatically that they've never claimed. It may have wrong hours, a wrong number, or reviews nobody has answered.

Wrong hours. A customer who drives to a closed business leaves a bad review about it. This is the most avoidable damage on the list.

No replies to reviews. Especially the negative ones. A calm, specific reply to a complaint reassures future customers far more than the absence of complaints would.

Address problems. Rules around virtual offices and shared addresses are strict, and suspensions happen. If you work from home or use a coworking address, understand the service-area business option before setting it up.

Common questions

Is Google Business Profile more important than a website?

For many local service businesses, yes, because the map results sit above the organic ones and answer a ready-to-buy customer's questions without them visiting a site. The website still matters for trust and for anything above a small purchase, but the profile is usually where the first contact happens.

How do I rank higher in Google Maps?

Google weighs proximity, relevance and prominence. Proximity you can't change. Relevance comes from a completely filled-in profile with the right primary category and matching website content. Prominence comes from steady reviews, real photos, and consistent name, address and phone details everywhere you're listed.

Should I reply to negative reviews?

Yes, calmly and specifically. Future customers read the reply more carefully than the complaint, and a measured response reassures them far more than an unblemished profile would. Leaving criticism unanswered is worse than receiving it.
