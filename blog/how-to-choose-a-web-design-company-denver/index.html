How to choose the best web design company in Denver

Search “best web design Denver” and you get lists written by the companies on them. Here is a way to judge any Denver web design firm yourself — including us — in about an afternoon.

9 min read · By Zach Wennstedt · Updated September 20, 2026

Short answer: The best web design company in Denver for you is the one whose own website loads fast, ranks for its own services, publishes prices, shows live work you can click, and tells you who will actually build your site. Check those five things before you look at a single portfolio mockup.

Every business owner I talk to has the same problem. There are dozens of Denver web design agencies, they all say “custom,” “strategic” and “results-driven,” and the top-ten lists are mostly paid placements. So you end up picking whoever called back first.

That's a bad way to spend several thousand dollars. Here's a better one.

Start with their own website

A web design company's own site is the only project where nobody told them no. No client asked for the ugly slider, no budget got cut. It's their best work, on their terms. So judge it hard.

Open it on your phone, not your laptop. That's where your customers will see your site. Does it load in a second or two? Can you find the phone number without hunting? Does anything jump around while it loads?

Run it through PageSpeed Insights. Google's free tool at pagespeed.web.dev scores the mobile experience. A web design firm whose own homepage scores badly on mobile is showing you what your site will score.

Search for them the way a customer would. Type “web design Denver” or the service they claim to specialize in. A company that can't rank its own site is going to have a hard time ranking yours.

Questions to ask on the first call

Who will actually build my site? Many agencies sell with a senior person and build with a junior one or an offshore team. That can be fine, but you should know.

What platform, and why that one for me? If the answer is always WordPress, or always Wix, the platform was decided before they heard about your business. We wrote a whole piece on static vs. WordPress, and the honest answer depends on how often you'll really edit the site.

Who owns the domain, the content and the code? Your domain should be registered in your name, full stop. Ask what happens to the site if you stop paying or leave.

What happens to my current rankings? If you have an existing site, a careless redesign can wipe out years of search progress. A good answer mentions redirects and keeping the URLs that already earn traffic. Our redesign checklist covers what should happen.

Will the site be readable by AI assistants? People now ask ChatGPT and Google's AI answers for recommendations. A 2026 website needs structured data and plain-language answers, not just keywords.

Red flags in a proposal

Guaranteed rankings. Nobody can guarantee a number-one spot on Google. Google's own guidance on hiring help says so. Anyone promising it is either guessing or planning to rank you for a phrase nobody searches.

No prices anywhere until a “discovery session.” Custom work does need a conversation, but a company that can't give you even a starting range is usually anchoring high.

A portfolio of mockups. Pretty screenshots of sites that were never launched, or that don't exist anymore. Ask for live links and click them.

Long contracts with no exit. Monthly plans are fine. Being unable to leave with your own domain and content isn't.

“SEO package” bolted on at the end. Search structure should be designed in from the first page, not sold as an add-on after launch. That's the whole idea behind SEO web design.

Agency, freelancer or DIY?

There isn't one right answer. A big Denver agency makes sense for a large company with a marketing team. A freelancer can be perfect for a simple site. A $5.99 builder is honestly fine for a brand-new business that needs something up this week. We compared the first two in freelance web designer vs. agency.

Common questions

How do I choose a web design company in Denver?

Check their own website on your phone, run it through Google PageSpeed Insights, search for their main service to see if they rank, look for published prices and live portfolio links, and ask who will actually build your site. A firm that passes those checks is worth a conversation.

How much do Denver web design companies charge?

Denver agencies commonly quote between $9,000 and $22,000 for a professional five-page site, while freelancers and smaller studios often charge less. Some studios, including ours, offer custom-coded sites from $99 per month with hosting and updates included.

Can a web design company guarantee first-page rankings?

No. No one can guarantee a number-one ranking on Google, and Google’s own guidance on hiring an SEO says so. Be cautious of any company that promises specific rankings.

Should my domain be registered in my name?

Yes. Your domain name should always be registered to you, not to your web designer. Ask about domain, content and code ownership before you sign anything.
