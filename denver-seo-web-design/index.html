The expensive mistake

Why bolting SEO on afterwards costs more

Here's the sequence that plays out constantly in Denver. A business hires a design shop. The site looks good. Six months later nobody has called, so they hire an SEO company. The SEO company opens the site and finds the URLs are a mess, the headings are decorative, there's no structured data, every service is on one page, and the whole thing is built on a theme that can't be made fast.

Now they're quoting to rebuild what you already paid for. That's not a scam — it's an honest assessment of a site that was never built to be found. You just paid twice for the same thing.

What breaks

URLs and structure

Five services crammed onto one page can't rank for five things. Splitting them later means new URLs, redirects, and starting the clock over.

What breaks

Speed you can't fix

If the site is a heavy theme with a plugin stack, speed work is triage. You can compress images all day and still be slow because the foundation is heavy.

What breaks

Content written for nobody

Beautiful copy that answers no question anybody typed. Rewriting it isn't editing — it's writing the site again.

The honest version: none of this means your current site is worthless. If it ranks for anything at all, that history has value and we'd rather keep it. Run a redesign assessment before assuming a rebuild. Sometimes the answer really is: fix four things and leave the rest alone.

Illustration of how structured data can present a result. Which enhancements Google actually shows is always Google's decision, and it varies by query and device — anyone promising you stars and FAQ dropdowns is promising something they don't control.

Being found by machines that answer

Search stopped being ten blue links

Somebody looking for a Denver contractor might never see a results page. They ask ChatGPT and get three recommendations. They see an AI Overview above the results and never scroll. They ask their phone out loud and hear one answer. Being invisible to those isn't a future problem.

What makes a site quotable

Language models are choosing what to cite. They favour pages that state facts plainly, answer a specific question near a heading that asks it, and carry structured data confirming who and where the business is. They struggle with pages that bury the answer in three paragraphs of atmosphere.

That is a writing discipline as much as a technical one. Short sentences. Real numbers. The question as a heading, the answer immediately underneath.

It also happens to be how people prefer to read. The optimization and the readability point in the same direction, which is rare.

What blocks it, usually by accident

A great many small business sites block AI crawlers without knowing. A security plugin adds the rule. A host enables it by default. A theme ships with a robots file nobody read. The result is that ChatGPT literally cannot see the business.

We check that on every build and allow the crawlers explicitly by name — GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, OAI-SearchBot, Google-Extended and the rest. We also publish an llms.txt file, a plain summary of the business written for machines to read.

What we do, and roughly when

The first six months, honestly

These are the phases of work, not a promise about rankings. Anybody who tells you they know when you'll hit page one is guessing, and charging you for the guess.

Week 1

Foundation

The site is built with structure already correct — URLs, headings, schema, speed, internal links. Search Console and a sitemap are set up so Google is told the site exists rather than left to stumble on it. This is the part most agencies bill separately.

Weeks 2–6

Indexing and baseline

Google crawls, indexes and starts forming an opinion. Early rankings appear, usually for your business name and very specific long phrases. Anything competitive is still nowhere. This stage feels like nothing is happening, and that is normal.

Months 2–4

Depth

More pages for more specific searches — individual services, individual suburbs, the questions your customers actually ask. Long-tail terms start converting first because there's less competition and more intent behind them.

Months 4–6

Authority and local

Google Business Profile, citations, consistent name-address-phone across the web, reviews, and links from places that matter. This is the slowest part and the hardest to shortcut. It's also what separates page one from page three on competitive local terms.

Ongoing

Maintenance and content

Search results shift, competitors publish, Google updates. A site left alone slides. Ongoing campaign work — content programs, link building, profile management — runs through Denver SEO at Eye To Ad Media, which is the same people.

Straight answers

Denver SEO web design questions

What is SEO web design?

Building a website so that search engines can find, read and rank it, at the same time as designing how it looks. The alternative is designing first and optimizing later, which usually means undoing structural decisions that were made without search in mind. Same work, different order, very different cost.

Do you charge separately for SEO?

Not for the structural side. Schema, headings, speed, URLs, sitemaps, crawler access and content written to answer real questions are part of every build, including sites at $99 a month. Ongoing campaign work — link building, Google Business Profile management, monthly content programs — is a separate service through Eye To Ad Media.

How long does SEO take to work in Denver?

Indexing happens within days to weeks. Rankings for specific long-tail phrases often appear within one to three months. Competitive local terms typically take six months or considerably longer, depending on who you're competing against and how established they are. Anybody giving you a precise date is guessing.

Can you guarantee first page rankings?

No, and neither can anyone else. Google's index isn't something an agency controls. What can be guaranteed is the work: the site will be technically correct, fast, structured, indexed and written to answer the questions your customers ask. Everything after that is Google's decision.

What is the difference between SEO and AI search optimization?

Considerable overlap and one real difference. Both need structured, fast, well-organized pages. AI search adds two things: crawler access for assistants like ChatGPT and Perplexity, and content written in a directly quotable form — a question as a heading with a plain answer underneath. Traditional SEO tolerates long preamble. AI systems mostly skip it.

Will a new website hurt my current rankings?

It can, if it's done carelessly. Changing URLs without redirects, dropping pages that rank, or losing content Google already values will cost you. Handled properly — mapping old URLs to new, keeping what performs, redirecting the rest — a rebuild usually improves things. This is the single most common way a redesign goes wrong.

Do I need a blog?

Only if you'll keep it going or pay someone to. A blog with four posts from 2023 is worse than no blog. Where it genuinely helps is answering the questions customers ask before they buy — costs, timelines, comparisons, what to look out for. Those pages get found and they close sales.

What about Google Business Profile?

For a local service business it's often more important than the website itself, because the map pack sits above organic results. A complete profile with real photos, correct hours, matching name-address-phone and steady reviews will outperform a lot of on-site work. It's part of the ongoing service, not the build.

How do I know if my current site has SEO problems?

Three free checks. Run Google's PageSpeed Insights on your homepage. Search Google for site:yourdomain.com and count how many pages are indexed — if it's far fewer than you have, something's wrong. Then search the exact service and city you want to rank for and see whether you appear anywhere at all.

Is SEO worth it for a small Denver business?

It depends entirely on whether people search for what you sell. A remodeler, a lawyer, a dentist or a plumber has real search demand and real intent behind it. A niche business selling to twelve companies in the state does not. Check whether anyone is searching before spending money on being found.
