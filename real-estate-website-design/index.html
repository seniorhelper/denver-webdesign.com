The myth, stated plainly

Thousands of listing pages will not make you rank

It's an appealing idea. Plug in the feed, get five thousand pages, watch the traffic arrive. It doesn't work, and it's worth understanding why before you buy a platform on that promise.

Those pages contain the same photographs, the same descriptions and the same specifications as every other IDX site pulling the same feed. Duplicate content at scale doesn't accumulate into authority. The listing sells, the agent changes, the page disappears, and whatever was building resets.

There is a narrow exception. A listing page with substantial original commentary — your honest read on the street, the school run, what the HOA is actually like — can rank for long-tail searches. But notice what's doing the work there. That's you writing, not the feed.

Straight answers

Real estate website questions

Do IDX listing pages help my SEO?

Not on their own. Those pages carry the same photos, descriptions and specs as every other agent site pulling the same feed, and duplicate content at scale doesn't build authority. The exception is a listing page with substantial original commentary you wrote — but that's your writing ranking, not the feed.

What actually generates leads on a real estate website?

Published research consistently points at home valuation tools, IDX property search with saved-search alerts, market reports, and contact forms on listing detail pages. Underneath all of those sits the content that brought the visitor there in the first place, which is the part most agents skip.

How much does a real estate website cost?

Our custom-coded sites start at $99 a month with no setup fee. IDX is a separate ongoing cost from whichever provider connects to your MLS, and pricing varies enormously between a simple search widget and a full platform with a CRM attached. Get both numbers before committing to either.

Do I need IDX at all?

If you work with buyers and you have content bringing people to the site, yes — it gives them a reason to return and register. If nobody currently visits your website, IDX is machinery with nothing to process. Build the content half first and add the feed once there's traffic to convert.

Should I force visitors to register before searching?

It does increase lead volume, and it also sends a share of visitors straight back to the big portals where registration isn't required. It's worth testing rather than assuming. Capturing at the moment somebody saves a search generally converts better than a wall at the door.

What content should I write?

The things only a local agent knows. What a neighbourhood is genuinely like to live in, what the commute is actually like, honest assessments including drawbacks. Six guides you wrote yourself will outperform sixty generated pages, because the generated ones exist identically on every competitor's site.

Can you connect IDX to a custom site?

Yes. The important question is how it's integrated — an embedded widget behaves differently from a proper API integration where listings live on your own domain. The second is more work and it's the version that contributes anything to your search visibility.

How long does it take to build a real estate website?

About a week for a straightforward agent site. IDX integration adds time because it depends on your MLS approving the feed and the provider connecting it, which is outside anyone's control. The content is usually the longest part, since neighbourhood guides have to be written rather than generated.

Do I need a blog as a real estate agent?

Only if you'll keep it going. A blog with four posts from two years ago reads worse than no blog. Where it genuinely works is neighbourhood guides and honest market commentary, published steadily. Those are the pages that earn search traffic and give an IDX search something to convert.

What is a home valuation tool and does it work?

A form where a homeowner enters their address and gets an estimate. It's consistently among the features most tied to lead generation because it captures sellers at the moment they're curious and before they've called anyone. Be honest about accuracy - an estimate that's wildly off costs you the credibility you just bought.

Should I use a real estate platform or a custom site?

Platforms bundle IDX, a CRM and hosting, which is convenient and means you don't own much. A custom site with a separate IDX feed means you own the domain, the content and the data, and can change providers without rebuilding. Convenience versus control, same trade as everywhere else.

Will my listings disappear when a property sells?

Yes, and that's part of why listing pages don't accumulate ranking value. The listing goes, the page goes, and whatever authority was building resets. Pages you write yourself - neighbourhood guides, market commentary, service areas - stay and compound.

Can I rank against Zillow and Realtor.com?

Not for broad searches like homes for sale in Denver. Those are settled. Where a local agent genuinely competes is specific, human queries the portals handle badly - what a particular neighbourhood is like to live in, honest local market reads, hyper-specific area questions. That's the gap worth working.

My brokerage gives me a website. Do I need my own?

The brokerage site is usually a profile page inside somebody else's domain, and it disappears if you move. Your own site with your own domain is an asset you keep. Worth having even if you also use whatever the brokerage provides.
