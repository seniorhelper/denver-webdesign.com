01 — The honest case

What Shopify is genuinely good at

We build on several platforms and recommend Shopify most often for stores, for reasons that have nothing to do with how it looks.

Payment security isn't your problem

Card data handling and compliance sit with Shopify. On a custom build that responsibility is yours, and it is not a small one.

Tax and shipping actually calculate

Rates by weight, destination and threshold, plus multi-state sales tax. Building this properly yourself is weeks of work you'd rather not pay for.

It doesn't break at 2am

Hosting, uptime and scaling during a rush are handled. Nobody calls you because a plugin update took the store down on a Saturday.

Abandoned carts recover themselves

Built in rather than bolted on. For most stores this single feature returns more revenue than a redesign would.

Inventory stays honest

Stock counts across variants, locations and channels. Overselling something you don't have is a costly, avoidable kind of bad review.

You can leave

Products and customers export. That matters more than people think when choosing where to build a business.

02 — The real decision

Customised theme or custom build?

This is the only Shopify decision that meaningfully changes your budget. Almost everything else is detail.

Customised theme

Most stores
  • + Live in about two to four weeks
  • + Costs a fraction of a custom front end
  • + Updates and security handled by the theme author
  • + Proven layouts that already convert
  • − Other stores use the same base
  • − Some layouts you simply can't have

Right for you if you're selling reasonably standard products and want to be trading this month rather than next quarter.

Custom build

Unusual needs
  • + Exactly the experience you designed
  • + No theme weight you aren't using
  • + Handles configurations no theme supports
  • − Significantly more expensive
  • − Longer to build and to change later
  • − Maintenance becomes your line item

Right for you if you have product configurators, wholesale tiers or bundles that themes genuinely cannot express — not just because custom sounds better.

For scale, published 2026 research puts full custom ecommerce builds on enterprise or headless architecture at $45,000 to $250,000. Most stores asking for "custom" want a well-customised theme and are relieved when someone says so. More on ecommerce platforms.

05 — Moving in

Migrating to Shopify without losing your traffic

The single most expensive mistake in a platform migration is changing every product URL and redirecting none of them. The store looks better and the orders stop, and it takes months to work out why.

Inventory every URL first

Every product, collection and page Google has indexed, plus which of them actually receive traffic. You cannot protect a list you never made.

Map old to new before building

Shopify's URL structure differs from WooCommerce, BigCommerce and most custom stores. Every old address needs a destination decided in advance.

Keep descriptions that rank

If a product page brings in traffic, its content is doing something. Rewriting it during a migration throws that away for no reason.

Move customers and history

Customer accounts, order history and reviews. Reviews in particular are hard-won and frequently lost in migrations because nobody exported them.

Redirect at launch, not later

Redirects go live with the store. A week of 404s on every product page is enough to do real damage that takes months to recover.

Watch Search Console after

Crawl errors show up within days. Assuming a migration went fine because the store loads is how problems get found in month three.

Same discipline as any website redesign — the platform changes, the principle doesn't.

06 — The other side

When Shopify is the wrong answer

You sell services, not products

A monthly platform fee for a checkout nobody uses. A normal website with a contact form or payment link does the job. Small business web design.

You publish far more than you sell

If the store is a small corner of a content-heavy site you post to weekly, WooCommerce on WordPress often fits better.

Under about ten orders a month

Payment links or invoices cover that without a subscription. Revisit when volume makes the fee obviously worth it.

Your pricing is quoted, not fixed

Custom quotes per customer don't fit a cart. What you need is a good website and a fast way to send a quote.

You're testing whether anyone wants it

A marketplace listing tells you that in a fortnight for almost nothing. Build the store once you know the answer.

The margin can't carry the fees

Subscription plus card processing plus apps is a real percentage of revenue. On thin margins and low order values, run the numbers before committing.

07 — Straight answers

Shopify questions

How much does a Shopify website cost to build?

A customised theme build is normal website territory and we quote per project, typically two to four weeks. A fully custom front end costs considerably more — published research puts full custom ecommerce at $45,000 to $250,000 at the enterprise end. Most stores want the first thing.

Do I need a custom theme or will a paid one do?

A well-customised paid theme suits the large majority of stores and gets you trading far sooner. Custom becomes worth it when you need product configurations, wholesale pricing tiers or bundles that themes genuinely cannot express. Wanting to look different is not usually enough reason on its own.

Why is my Shopify store slow?

Almost always three things: too many apps each loading their own scripts, oversized product images, and a heavy theme with sliders and video. Run PageSpeed Insights on a product page, not the homepage — that's where your customers actually land and where the weight usually is.

How many apps is too many?

There's no fixed number, but every app typically adds scripts to every page. The useful test is whether each one earns its weight and its monthly fee. Most stores we look at have at least two nobody uses and one duplicating a feature the theme already has.

Can you migrate my store from WooCommerce or another platform?

Yes. The critical part is mapping every existing product URL to its new address and publishing redirects at launch, so the traffic that was already buying still arrives. Reviews and customer history need exporting deliberately too, because they're commonly lost.

Will my products show up in Google?

They can, with product schema so price and availability appear in results, unique descriptions rather than the manufacturer's copy, and collection pages written for how people search. Duplicate supplier text across a thousand stores is the most common reason product pages don't rank.

Do you build Shopify apps?

We build custom functionality for stores, which sometimes means a private app and sometimes means theme-level code that avoids needing one. The second is usually cheaper and lighter. More on custom development.

How long until my store is live?

Two to four weeks for most stores. Almost all of the variance is product data — photos, descriptions, variants and weights for everything you sell. A fifty-product store with no photos ready takes considerably longer than the build itself would suggest.

Do you work with stores outside Denver?

Yes. Ecommerce has no geography. Most clients are in the Denver metro and we also work along the Front Range, including Monument, as well as out of state.

What are the ongoing costs?

Platform subscription, card processing on every order at typically 2.9% plus 30 cents, and any app subscriptions. There's a calculator for that — put your own numbers in and see what it comes to per order and per year.
