The real question

If your website isn't bringing you customers, what is it for?

Most small business websites are digital business cards. They sit there. They look fine. They do nothing. Nobody finds them on Google, nobody calls, and the owner quietly decides the internet doesn't work for their industry.

That isn't a design problem. It's a signals problem. A website earns customers when search engines can read it, AI assistants can quote it, phones can load it fast, and visitors can figure out what to do in about four seconds. Miss any one of those and the prettiest site in Denver still makes your phone stay quiet.

Symptom

Symptom

Most of your visitors are on a phone with one bar of signal. A heavy site loses a third of them before the first image appears.

What we build

Four ways to build a website. We do all four.

Most Denver shops sell one platform because it's the only one they know. Then every client gets that platform whether it fits or not. Here's the honest version of each, including where ours is the wrong choice.

Static HTML — our default, and why

No database, no plugins, no admin login. The pages are already built, so the server just hands them over. That makes them extremely fast, effectively unhackable, and cheap to host.

Best for: service businesses, contractors, law firms, clinics, restaurants, anyone whose site is mainly pages and a contact form.

Wrong choice when: you need to publish new content several times a week yourself without asking anyone. Then you want a CMS.

WordPress — when you need to edit it yourself

The most widely used platform on the internet, and we build on it when it's the right call. It gives you a login, an editor, and the ability to post whenever you want.

Best for: businesses publishing frequent blog content, or teams with multiple people updating the site.

The trade-off, stated plainly: WordPress needs maintenance. Plugins update, break, and occasionally open security holes. Nearly every hacked small business site we've been called to fix was hacked through a plugin. If you choose WordPress, budget for upkeep — or let us handle it.

Shopify — real ecommerce with real payments

If you're selling physical products with inventory, shipping and card payments, use a platform built for it. Shopify handles the parts that are genuinely hard: payment security, tax, inventory sync, abandoned carts.

Best for: product businesses, anyone processing more than a handful of orders a week.

Wrong choice when: you sell services and just want a contact form. You'd be paying a monthly platform fee for a shopping cart you never use.

Drag-and-drop builders — the $5.99 option

We offer these too, starting around $5.99 a month, and we'll be straight with you: it's the option we recommend least. You build it yourself from a template, and it will look like a template. Search performance is usually weak because you can't control the underlying code.

Best for: a brand-new business that needs something online this week for almost nothing, with plans to replace it later.

Wrong choice when: the website is meant to bring in customers. Which is usually the entire point.

Security

There are no plugins to hack

Small business websites don't usually get targeted personally. They get swept up by bots scanning the whole internet for one known vulnerability in one outdated plugin. That's how a plumbing company in Aurora ends up serving pharmacy spam.

A static site removes the attack surface instead of defending it. No database to inject. No login page to brute force. No plugin directory to exploit. HTTPS on every page, security headers set, forms filtered for spam before they reach a human.

It's not that we secure static sites better. It's that there's far less there to secure.

No database

Nothing to inject, nothing to dump, nothing to hold for ransom.

No admin login

No password for a bot to guess at three in the morning.

No plugin chain

The most common way small business sites get compromised simply isn't present.

No. We build static HTML sites, WordPress sites, and Shopify stores, and we'll tell you which one actually fits. Static is our default because it's faster and has no plugins to be hacked through. WordPress makes sense when you need to publish new content yourself on a regular basis.

Will my website show up in ChatGPT and AI search?

It can, and it's a real part of how we build. AI assistants read structured data, clear question-and-answer content, and sites that allow their crawlers access. Most small business websites accidentally block AI crawlers or give them nothing quotable. We do the opposite, on every build.

Denver and the surrounding metro is our home market and where most of our clients are. We do work with businesses elsewhere in Colorado and out of state. Local matters most when your customers search with local intent, because that's where knowing the market makes a measurable difference.

Can you fix or rebuild my current website?

Yes, and it's often the faster path. If you already have traffic and rankings, we'd rather improve what's working than throw it out. If the current site is slow, unindexed or built on something unmaintainable, a rebuild usually costs less than repeatedly patching it.
