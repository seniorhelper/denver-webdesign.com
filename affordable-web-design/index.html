Where cheap gets expensive

Six ways a bargain website costs you more

None of these are arguments against spending less. They're the specific places where spending less turns into spending twice.

Trap 01

The setup fee you find at the end

An attractive monthly price with a build fee revealed once you're invested in the conversation. Ask for the total first-year cost in writing before anything else. Ours has no setup fee and that's on the pricing page, not held back.

Trap 02

The domain in somebody else's name

The single worst outcome on this list. If the designer registers your domain to themselves, leaving becomes a negotiation. Your domain should be registered to you from day one. Check it before you pay anybody.

Trap 03

Edits billed by the hour

A cheap build with $95-an-hour changes. New phone number, new service, updated hours — you'll need dozens of those over the years, and most people stop bothering, which is worse than the cost.

Trap 04

Nobody thought about search

A site with no structure has to be partly rebuilt before any marketing works. That's paying twice for the same thing, and it's the most common reason a cheap site turns out expensive.

Trap 05

Maintenance that never happens

On WordPress, no maintenance means an outdated plugin eventually gets found by an automated scan. Cleaning up a compromised site costs far more than the upkeep would have.

Trap 06

The developer who disappears

More common than you'd think. If you can't reach them and don't have hosting or domain access, recovery is possible but slow and irritating. Ask who else can get in before you need to know.

Affordable web design questions

What is the cheapest way to get a business website?

A drag-and-drop builder plan, roughly $5.99 a month on the plans we resell, built by you. It's genuinely the cheapest real option. Be honest about whether the site is a formality or is supposed to generate customers, because builders are limited at the second job.

Is a cheap website bad for SEO?

Not automatically, but template builders limit what you can control — page structure, speed, schema, URL patterns. A cheap site built with those in mind beats an expensive one built without. What matters is control over the code, not the price tag.

How can you build custom sites for $99 a month?

Low overhead and a monthly model. There's no office, no account manager and no sales team to fund, and spreading the cost over time means we don't need a five-figure invoice up front. It also keeps us responsible after launch, because the site still has to be working in year two.

What's the catch with cheap web design?

Usually one of six things: a setup fee revealed late, your domain registered to somebody else, edits billed hourly, no search structure at all, no maintenance, or a developer who disappears. Ask about each of those specifically and most of the risk goes away.

Should I use Wix, Squarespace or something similar?

They're solid products and we sell builder plans ourselves. They suit businesses where the website is a formality or where you genuinely want to do it yourself. Where they struggle is competitive local search, because you can't control enough of the underlying code.

Can I start cheap and upgrade later?

Yes, and plenty of people do exactly that. Start on a builder, prove the business works, move to a custom build when it can carry it. The only thing to protect is your domain — keep it registered to you so moving is straightforward.

Do you offer discounts or payment plans?

The monthly pricing is the discount, in effect — it removes the upfront barrier that stops most small businesses getting a decent site at all. Larger projects get quoted individually and structured sensibly for the scope.

What if I can't afford anything right now?

Then don't buy a website. Claim your Google Business Profile, make your phone number tappable, and run the free checks. Call and we'll walk you through it anyway. We'd rather you came back in six months able to afford it than took on cost you can't carry.
