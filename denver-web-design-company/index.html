The honest market map

Four kinds of Denver web design company

There are roughly four types of shop you can hire in this city. Each is genuinely right for somebody. Here's where we fit and where we don't.

The full-service agency — $9,000 to $22,000+

Twenty to thirty people, an office in LoDo or Cherry Creek, an account manager between you and the work. Real strategy, real process, real overhead. Published Denver pricing guides put a professional five-page site in the $9,000–$22,000 range, and higher-end custom projects considerably above that.

Right for you if: you're a mid-market company with a marketing department, multiple stakeholders and a budget that treats the website as a capital expense.

Wrong for you if: you're a contractor with a truck and a phone. You'd be funding an office you'll never visit.

The offshore shop — $1,000 to $4,000

Cheap, fast, and sometimes technically good. The trade is time zones, communication, and whether anyone picks up when something breaks in eighteen months.

Right for you if: you know exactly what you want, you can write a tight spec, and you don't need much support afterward.

Wrong for you if: you want somebody who knows what a Denver customer searching at 7am actually types.

The DIY builder — $0 to $30 a month

You build it yourself from a template. We sell these too, from $5.99 a month, and we'll tell you honestly it's the option we recommend least when the site is meant to generate business.

Right for you if: you need something online this week for almost nothing and you'll replace it later.

Wrong for you if: you want to be found. Template sites are hard to optimize because you don't control the code underneath.

The small local shop — where we sit

One or a few people who do the design, the code and the search work themselves. Lower overhead, so lower price. You talk to the person doing the work, which means fewer misunderstandings and faster changes.

Right for you if: you're a local business that needs the phone to ring and doesn't want to fund a marketing department to get there.

Wrong for you if: you need twelve people available simultaneously across four time zones. We're not that, and we'd rather say so now.

Straight answers

Questions about hiring a Denver web design company

What should a Denver web design company cost?

It depends entirely on which of the four types you hire. Full-service Denver agencies commonly quote $9,000 to $22,000 for a professional five-page site. Offshore shops run $1,000 to $4,000. DIY builders are a few dollars a month. Our custom-coded sites start at $99 per month, and builder plans start at $5.99. None of those numbers is wrong — they're buying different things.

Should I hire a local Denver company or a remote one?

For a business whose customers are in Denver, local knowledge genuinely helps — knowing which suburbs matter, how people here search, what the competition already ranks for. For a business selling nationally it matters much less. Be honest about which one you are, because paying a local premium for national work is wasted money.

How do I know if a web design company is any good?

Open their own website on your phone and see how fast it loads. Run it through Google's free PageSpeed Insights tool. Search for the terms they claim to rank for and see whether they show up. A company that can't get its own site found is telling you something about what yours will look like.

What questions should I ask before hiring?

Who is actually doing the work. What happens after launch. Whether the price includes hosting, updates and search structure. Who owns the domain and the content. What the timeline depends on. And what happens if you want to leave. Any company that gets vague on those is answering the question.

Do you work with businesses outside Denver?

Yes. Denver and the surrounding metro is home and where most clients are, and we work across Colorado and out of state as well. The build is the same either way; local knowledge just matters more when your customers are searching with local intent.

Can you take over a website another company built?

Usually. It depends on what it's built on and whether you have access to the domain and hosting. Bring us the login details and we'll tell you honestly whether it's worth keeping or whether a rebuild costs less than the ongoing repairs.
