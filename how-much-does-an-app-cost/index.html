Spending less

Four ways to cut the number honestly

Biggest saving

Build the MVP first

A working version with only the core feature costs $15,000 to $35,000 and gives you real user data before you commit to a full build. The features people request after launch are almost never the ones assumed during planning — which means a full build is partly wasted money by definition.

Real saving

Cross-platform instead of two native builds

One codebase for iOS and Android rather than two. Suits most business apps comfortably. Separate native builds are worth it when performance or deep device features genuinely matter, and not otherwise.

Often overlooked

Ask whether it needs to be an app

A progressive web app installs to the home screen, works offline and skips the stores entirely, at a fraction of the cost. For plenty of businesses it delivers most of what they wanted. More on that here.

Free

Cut features before you get quotes

Vendors quote what you ask for. Every feature you can defer to version two comes off the price and off the timeline. Scope discipline before quoting is worth more than negotiating after.

The question worth answering first

Would your customer open this more than once a month? If not, you almost certainly want a fast mobile website rather than an app, because an app requires somebody to find it in a store and install it before you can reach them at all. That's a large barrier for something used twice a year.

We'd rather tell you that before you spend $50,000 than after. A website may be the answer.

Straight answers

App cost questions

How much does it cost to build an app in 2026?

Published 2026 ranges put a simple app or MVP at $10,000 to $25,000, moderate apps with custom APIs and logins at $25,000 to $75,000, complex multi-platform builds at $75,000 to $150,000, and enterprise projects at $150,000 to $300,000 or more. Industry data across 5,000+ projects puts the average custom mobile app at around $171,000, with most small-to-mid business apps between $50,000 and $120,000.

What are the ongoing costs after launch?

Maintenance typically runs 15 to 20 percent of the build cost every year, and year-one post-launch work commonly lands between $15,000 and $50,000. Add an annual Apple developer fee, a one-off Google Play registration, backend infrastructure and any third-party API subscriptions. None of this usually appears in a build quote.

Why do quotes vary so much for the same app?

Because vendors don't quote the same scope. One includes design, testing, backend and store submission; another quotes the front end alone. Before comparing numbers, ask each vendor exactly what's included and what happens after launch. Otherwise you're comparing figures that describe different projects.

What is an MVP and should I build one?

A working version with only the core feature that solves the central problem. It costs $15,000 to $35,000 and gives you real user data before you commit to a full build. Most successful apps start this way, because the features users actually request after launch are almost always different from the ones assumed during planning.

How long does it take to build an app?

Two to nine months depending on complexity and team capacity. Store review adds time at the end and rejections are routine rather than a sign of a problem. The variable that moves the timeline most is how quickly decisions get made on your side.

Is it cheaper to build for one platform first?

It can be, but cross-platform development usually serves business apps better — one codebase covering both stores. Separate native builds make sense when performance or deep device features genuinely matter. Launching iOS-only to save money sometimes just delays the Android cost rather than removing it.

Can AI tools build my app for less?

They've genuinely lowered the floor, and some sources report AI-accelerated builds landing well below the traditional range. What they don't remove is scoping, testing, security, store compliance and maintenance. Treat dramatic savings claims as applying to the build phase only, because that's the part they actually shorten.

Do I even need an app?

Ask whether customers would open it more than once a month. If not, a fast mobile website almost always serves better, because an app requires them to find it in a store and install it first. Apps earn their place through repeat use, and that's the honest test before spending anything.
